NSF FINDERS FOUNDRY · Planning phase, 2026

Classroom AI that helps students do their own thinking

We are designing an AI for middle school engineering that shows how it works, says what it can't do, and asks questions instead of handing out answers. Students observe nature, sketch in a paper journal, and invent something inspired by what they find. The AI is one small part of their week.

Try the walkthrough Watch the videos

The problem

A correct, friendly answer can still skip the learning

Students increasingly hand their thinking to AI. Most AI tools work as a black box: the answer appears, but the reasoning stays hidden.

Black box

Gives a finished answer, offers to write the assignment, and hides how it got there.

Glass box

Shows its confidence and sources, names its limits ("I can only see pixels"), and hands the thinking back to the student with questions.

Design themes · v3.1, September 2026

Eleven themes that shape the design

The team consolidated its sessions, reviewer feedback, and user story comments into eleven themes. Each was tested with parents and teachers in the focus groups.

  1. Glass box: the AI shows its thinking on the live task, through instruments, not engine

    Gauges for confidence and sources, plus an optional "X-ray" view, show what the AI is doing on this task.

  2. A machine, not a friend, but one that genuinely listens

    A robot persona states its limits when they matter and restates what the student actually said.

  3. The intelligence is the student's, and the shared load stays visible

    The AI asks, points to readings, and defines terms but never authors the student's work. Tags show whose ideas are whose.

  4. The body does the learning

    Students go outside, observe with senses a camera lacks, and sketch before uploading anything.

  5. The companion journal carries the structure

    A paper field journal holds noticings, sketches, and designs, and links class sessions through homework.

  6. Conversational pacing: short turns, many of them, every question answered

    Two to three sentences per AI turn, a cap on AI turns before the student acts, and no rhetorical questions.

  7. Structure and agency: chapters, levels, and a map you can move around on

    Short chapters, light leveling, and a design-cycle map students can move back through to revise.

  8. Teachers keep professional judgment, and get their own instruments

    A teacher view shows where students are and who is stuck, with suggested ways to step in.

  9. Inclusive by design

    A biology-first entry point, grade-level curated readings, and no timed tasks that favor fast typers.

  10. Responsive instruction and assessment built in

    Targeted follow-up questions, plans for off-track replies, and formative assessment at each level.

  11. AI literacy and workforce content as a pipeline

    Explainers on how neural networks and generative AI work, and the people who build AI systems.

Standards

Aligned with middle school engineering design

The "Biology to Design" lesson addresses the NGSS middle school engineering design standards and two NGSS crosscutting concepts, with Common Core literacy connections. Its AI literacy strand aligns with the AI4K12 Five Big Ideas and the AILit framework.

StandardHow the lesson addresses itAI literacy (AI4K12 grades 6-8; AILit)
Asking Questions and Defining Problems (MS-ETS1-1)Students define a design problem by connecting a nature-inspired idea to a human need, with criteria and constraints shaped by scientific knowledge.AI4K12 5-A-i: students compare how human needs shape their designs with how stakeholders' goals shape AI systems. AILit Engage with AI: students weigh the AI's questions and suggestions.
ETS1.A: Defining and Delimiting Engineering Problems (MS-ETS1-1)Criteria and constraints shape each design; one required criterion is that the solution is bioinspired.AI4K12 1-A-i, 1-A-ii: students contrast their own multisensory observation with the AI's camera-only sensing. AILit Manage AI: students decide which parts of the task the AI is suited for.
ETS1.B: Developing Possible Solutions (MS-ETS1-2, MS-ETS1-3)Students evaluate their design concept against criteria and constraints, including how the biological function works.AI4K12 2-A-iv: word embeddings explain how the AI matches a nature story to an idea; 3-C-iii: training data shapes what the AI suggests. AILit Engage with AI.
Influence of Science, Engineering, and Technology on Society and the Natural World (MS-ETS1-1)Designs respond to human needs and draw on the findings of scientific research.AI4K12 5-B-i, 5-C-ii: how AI is changing daily life and work, including people who build AI. AILit Shape AI.
Systems and System Models (crosscutting concept)Students explain a living thing's ability as a system of interacting parts, then design their invention as a system that meets criteria and constraints.AI4K12 1-B-iv, 3-B-i: the AI itself as a system of interacting parts, from feature detectors to a neural network. AILit Shape AI.
Common Core ELA/LiteracyRST.6-8.1, RST.6-8.7, RST.6-8.9, WHST.6-8.7, WHST.6-8.8, WHST.6-8.9, SL.8.5AI4K12 1-B-iii: how a computer recognizes phrases from sequences of words. AILit Engage with AI: checking the sources behind an AI reply.

AI4K12 codes refer to grade 6-8 learning objectives in the AI4K12 progression charts. AILit domains are from the AI Literacy Framework of the European Commission and the OECD.
